2. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

2.1 The Problem Context [max 250 words]

AI is changing which jobs exist faster than training programmes can be updated. In Malaysia, 56% of professionals already use AI tools at work, yet only 26% have received formal training (Hays Asia Salary Guide 2025), and the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation expects more than 600,000 workers to need reskilling within three to five years. The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 (Malaysia profile) projects that the share of work tasks done mainly by people will fall from 47% to 33% by 2030, and that data-entry clerk jobs will shrink by 28% while AI and machine-learning specialist jobs grow by 29%.

The people most affected are not short of information about AI; they are short of a personal, forward-looking answer. A fresh graduate choosing a first job, an admin executive whose spreadsheet work is being automated, or a final-year engineering student cannot tell which of their own skills AI will take over, which will keep them valuable, and what single thing to learn next. Existing career tools give a one-time keyword match against a static list, and "AI career coaches" are chatbots that can invent jobs or courses.

This matters economically (SDG 8: productive employment) and socially (SDG 4: relevant skills and lifelong learning), most of all for workers without paid career advice. The specific moment we design for is: a person deciding what to learn next, before AI makes that decision for them.

2.2 The Proposed AI Solution [max 300 words]

Future Edge turns a CV into a three-year career forecast, shown as a weather forecast: an Edge Score from 0 to 100 for each year, meaning "how hard you will be to replace by AI". A user goes from CV to ranked roles and an ordered learning plan in about three minutes.

Three models work together:

  • Model 1 – AI exposure: a text classifier trained on 19,265 human-rated O*NET work tasks (Eloundou et al., 2023) reads each skill and the user's own CV sentence and estimates how much of that work AI can do (73.4% accuracy on occupations it never saw, versus 53.2% for a simple baseline).
  • Model 2 – AI-trend signal: monthly arXiv paper counts for ten AI-capability areas, forecast with a damped trend and an 80% range, decide how fast AI tools arrive for each skill. Refreshing the data live changes the forecast.
  • Model 3 – Catch the AI: instead of a self-rating quiz, the user reviews an AI-written work sample with planted mistakes. This measures judgment and responsibility, the skills that keep humans in charge of AI.

These combine with real Malaysian job-market data (WEF Future of Jobs 2025 Malaysia profile, DOSM wages, TalentCorp MyCOL critical occupations) into an explainable score. Every role and skill is tagged to SDG targets, the user sees moderate and fast AI-disruption scenarios side by side, and the plan starts with the "One Move" that raises their score most.

No generative model writes recommendations, so the system cannot invent jobs, courses or statistics. From the SDC Phase 1 workshops we use Replit for development and hosting and structured outputs (a JSON schema) when the optional Gemini API reads hard-to-parse CVs; every returned phrase is checked against the CV text.

3.2 Design-Thinking & Deployment Strategy [max 200 words]

Empathise: we wrote three realistic personas from the problem statement – a final-year engineering student, an admin executive with six years' experience and a fresh CS graduate – and built synthetic CVs for each. Define: their shared question was not "which jobs match my keywords?" but "which of my skills will still be mine in three years, and what do I learn first?" Ideate: we rejected a chatbot (it can invent jobs and courses) in favour of a forecast built from measurable signals, and replaced self-rating quizzes with a test that has known answers. Prototype and test: every model has a held-out evaluation, 49 automated tests guard the scoring rules, and every course link was checked on 24 October 2026. Testing showed that a plain trend over-shot, so we switched to a damped trend; it also showed leadership being flagged "at risk", so human skills now gain AI exposure more slowly.

Deployment readiness: the prototype is already live on Replit. For Phase 2 we will run a user test with XMUM students, add a Bahasa Malaysia interface and an employer team view, and keep the same deployment pipeline.
